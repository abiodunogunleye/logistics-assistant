# Northbank Logistics — SOP: Damaged Goods Handling

**Document ID:** SOP-DG-01
**Version:** 1.0
**Owner:** Warehouse Operations
**Last reviewed:** 2026-10-02

## Purpose
Defines how to identify, quarantine, report, and resolve damaged stock found
anywhere in the warehouse, so damaged items never reach a customer and losses
are recorded accurately.

## Scope
Applies to all warehouse operatives and shift supervisors at any Northbank
Logistics site, covering damage found during goods-in, putaway, picking,
packing, or routine stock checks.

## Procedure

### 1. Identifying Damage
1. Treat any item with a torn, crushed, wet, or opened outer as damaged until
   inspected, even if the contents look fine.
2. Check for leaking, expired, or visibly contaminated product on food,
   chemical, or liquid SKUs.
3. If unsure whether an item counts as damaged, treat it as damaged and
   escalate rather than guess.

### 2. Quarantine
1. Remove the damaged item from the pick face or receiving area immediately.
2. Move it to the designated damaged-goods quarantine area — never leave it
   in a live bin or on the pack bench.
3. Attach a damaged-goods tag stating SKU, quantity, date, and who found it.

### 3. Reporting
1. Log the damage in the incident system (or paper log if the system is
   down) within 15 minutes of discovery.
2. Record SKU, quantity, damage type, and where it was found.
3. Take a photo of the damage where the system supports photo attachments.
4. Flag the shift supervisor for high-value SKUs or damage affecting more
   than one unit of the same batch.

### 4. Resolution
1. Supervisor decides whether the item is written off, returned to supplier,
   or downgraded for internal use, per current stock policy.
2. Update stock records to remove written-off quantity from available stock
   the same day.
3. File supplier-fault damage against the relevant purchase order for
   recharge or replacement.

### 5. Prevention
1. If damage is repeatedly linked to a specific racking location, handling
   method, or supplier packaging, raise it with the shift supervisor as a
   layout or packaging issue rather than logging it as a one-off each time.

## Reporting / Escalation
- Any damage affecting food safety or chemical containment: notify the shift
  supervisor immediately, not at end of shift.
- Repeated damage from the same supplier: raise with the warehouse manager
  for supplier follow-up.

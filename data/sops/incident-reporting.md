# Northbank Logistics — SOP: Incident Reporting

**Document ID:** SOP-IR-01
**Version:** 1.2
**Owner:** Warehouse Operations
**Last reviewed:** 2026-10-06

## Purpose
Defines what counts as a reportable incident, how to report one, and how
incidents are reviewed, so issues are caught consistently and acted on.

## Scope
Applies to all warehouse operatives, First Line Managers (FLMs), Senior FLMs,
and Shift Managers at any Northbank Logistics site.

## Definitions
These definitions apply to every Northbank Logistics SOP.
- **High-value item:** an item with a unit value over £500. High-value SKUs
  are marked high_value = yes in the stock list (data/stock.csv).
- **Safety-critical item:** chemicals, aerosols, lithium batteries, food, and
  anything labelled as hazardous.
- **Large discrepancy:** a difference of more than 5% of the expected
  quantity, or more than 10 units. Either one is enough.
- **Significant stock loss:** a single incident with a total stock loss value
  over £1,000. The Senior FLM reviews the incident logs weekly for repeated
  smaller losses from the same cause.

## What Counts as an Incident
- Any injury or near-miss involving a person, however minor.
- Equipment damage or malfunction (forklifts, pallet trucks, racking, etc.).
- Stock damage, loss, or discrepancy beyond normal picking error correction.
- Any safety hazard such as a spill, blocked fire exit, or racking damage.

## Procedure

### 1. Reporting
1. Make the immediate area safe first — isolate equipment, cordon off a
   spill, or clear people from danger — before filing the report.
2. Log the incident in the incident system (or paper log if the system is
   down) as soon as the area is safe, and no later than the end of the
   shift. Where another SOP sets a shorter deadline for a specific task, the
   shorter deadline takes priority — for example, 5 minutes for picking
   errors (SOP-PE-01), or 15 minutes for damaged goods and goods-in
   discrepancies (SOP-DG-01, SOP-GI-01).
3. Record what happened, where, when, who was involved, and any immediate
   action taken.
4. For injuries, also record whether first aid was given and by whom.

### 2. Immediate Escalation
1. For any injury, alert your FLM or a first aider immediately. Make sure
   your FLM knows, even if a first aider has dealt with it.
2. Notify your FLM immediately for any fire, chemical spill, or equipment
   failure that could put people at risk.
3. The FLM informs the Shift Manager straight away for any injury needing
   medical attention, fire, or chemical spill.
4. Do not wait for the end-of-shift review to escalate anything safety
   critical.

### 3. FLM Review
1. The FLM reviews all incidents logged in their area before the shift ends.
2. Confirm any immediate hazard has been fully resolved, not just reported.
3. Escalate to the Senior FLM the same shift for significant stock loss (see
   Definitions) or equipment taken out of service.

### 4. Near-Miss Reporting
1. Report near misses (something that could have caused harm but didn't)
   using the same process as incidents — they are not optional to log.
2. Near misses are reviewed weekly by the Shift Manager with the Senior FLMs
   to spot patterns before they become real incidents.

## Reporting / Escalation
- Injuries requiring medical attention: alert your FLM or a first aider
  immediately. The FLM informs the Shift Manager straight away.
- Fires or chemical spills: notify your FLM immediately. The FLM informs the
  Shift Manager straight away.
- Significant stock loss or equipment out of service: the FLM escalates to
  the Senior FLM the same shift.
- Repeated near misses in the same area or task: raised at the weekly
  Shift Manager review for a layout, equipment, or training change.
- If your FLM is unavailable, go to the Senior FLM.
